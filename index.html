The Story

How the world rewired music

Four chapters, one thread

Over four decades, music stopped being local. Borders dissolved, not only in how songs travel but in how they actually sound. Crises, digital connection and cultural collision rewired what the world listens to, how it's made, and how it feels.

01 · Sankey Diagram Who makes the music, and where?

Cultural Flow

In 1986 the map was simple: genres stayed close to home. Four decades on, Pop has colonised the planet, swelling from 30% to 48.3% of everything we stream. The quiet twist? The closer you look, the more Europe grows, expanding its share instead of vanishing into North America's shadow.

Global Collabs

Then the borders dissolved. Artists stopped needing the same studio, the same city, even the same continent, and collaboration across regions exploded. The US and UK axis alone anchors 308 partnerships across 495 songs, parking Europe at the centre of the world's busiest creative pipeline. The internet didn't just move music. It started writing it together.

Resonance Timeline

Here the data starts to feel. Happiness peaked in 1999 at 62.7% valence, just as Europe launched the Euro and rode a wave of optimism, right before the crash. It hit the floor in 2015, the year of the refugee crisis and the Paris attacks. Yet as the world grew sadder, the music grew more danceable: people reached for rhythm when joy slipped out of reach.

Genre Forecast

Push the curves forward and the genres swap places. Electronic climbs 18.4%, Hip Hop slides 14.2%, and Rock is on course to flatline by 2026. Electronic's rise is really Europe's rise: the continent of Berlin techno and Ibiza nights watching its native sound seize the global stage. The natural evolution of a connected culture, or the loss of something? The line keeps moving.

The thread

One variable runs beneath all four: globalisation. It's why Pop dominates, why Europe keeps gaining ground, why collaborations exploded, why the sound shifted, and why the genres are converging. Not four visualisations, but one argument, told in four chapters.
